Vanny Vabiola is an Indonesian singer.

You might wonder why I love Indonesian songs. Not many Taiwanese people listen to them—in fact, I don't know anyone who does. I don't speak Indonesian either. I hope to take an Indonesian course next year, as long as it doesn't clash with my required classes. We just have way too many required courses in Life Science...

# YouTube's auto-play

As I kept playing Vanny's English covers, YouTube started recommending her other songs. Sometimes I turned on auto-play while washing dishes or riding the bicycle, and YouTube would play Indonesian songs I couldn't understand.

I wasn't used to the style and didn't like it at first. But my hands were busy with dishes so I couldn't skip them. YouTube thought I liked those songs, so it kept playing more Indonesian tracks, thinking it was right! It was a total systematic misunderstanding.

However, once you listen enough, you get used to it. And once you get used to it, you can start to like it. After all, Vanny's voice itself is beautiful.

# The Minang style

Indonesia is huge, with nearly 300 million people spread across over a thousand islands. Therefore, its dialects and cultures are highly diverse and complex. Vanny's Indonesian songs are in the Minang style. The Minang language region is located on Sumatra Island in western Indonesia, where Vanny was born.

---

# The Minang style

In comparison, the melodies of western cover songs are like the cross-section of a trachea. Minang style songs are like the cross-section of a small intestine, using vocal ripples and waves to express deep emotions.

Indonesian is her mother tongue, so she pours much more emotion into it. I choose different songs to match my mood.

# Let's listen to foreign songs

I love songs with lyrics I don't understand. They let me appreciate the vocals without the meaning of the words distracting from the music.

Looking back, I first started liking English songs because I didn't understand the lyrics. But as I gradually learned English, that benefit disappeared. Now that I listen to Indonesian songs, I found that joy again. If I ever learn Indonesian, I might have to switch to another language.

Another benefit of foreign songs is that you can listen to them while studying. Since you don't understand them, they won't trigger the language centers of your brain, distract you, or interrupt your learning.

You should try it too. Go discover some foreign songs you don't understand. It will be a very interesting experience.
